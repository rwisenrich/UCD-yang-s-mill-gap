# Referee audit: regulator identity and theorem boundaries

This note records the distinctions that a Jaffe-Witten referee will test first.

## 1. Proof regulator vs finite quantum-link workbench

The continuum proof regulator is `L2(G)` on each link, with the Wilson or heat-kernel compact-group plaquette action. The 20-dimensional three-rishon SU(3) link is a finite algebraic workbench used to verify gauge covariance, Schur reduction, transfer matrices and contraction bounds. It is not substituted for `L2(SU(3))`.

The bridge between finite computation and the proof regulator is the Peter-Weyl sequence

`H_K = direct_sum_{p+q<=K} V_(p,q) tensor V_(p,q)^*`,

whose union is dense in `L2(SU(3))`.

## 2. H504 gap vs Yang-Mills gap

The exact H504 ring theorem `Delta_504 = 2 kappa exp(-1/72)` is a finite-registry theorem. The Yang-Mills mass gap is the continuum spectral statement `Spec(H) cap (0,Delta)=empty`. The H504 result is used for exact Schur architecture, not as a relabeled continuum mass.

## 3. What the quadratic Schur theorem establishes

The theorem proves a uniform gap floor for any exact block RG tower satisfying the stated scale-uniform Q-sector separation and local-path norm hypotheses. The finite SU(3) workbench supplies a stress-test point satisfying the inequalities with a conservative 4D two-step path count.

Application to continuum Yang-Mills requires proving that the exact compact-group Wilson/heat-kernel RG enters that basin with scale- and volume-uniform constants.

## 4. Continuum reconstruction

Reflection positivity is available at the Wilson regulator and is closed under convergent limits. The substantive continuum construction is therefore concentrated in convergence/renormalization of gauge-invariant Schwinger distributions, Euclidean-symmetry restoration, clustering and nontriviality.

## 5. Submission criterion

A final theorem package is complete when every primary `OPEN_*` row in `results/jaffe_witten_obligation_ledger_v4.csv` has been replaced by a proved proposition with explicit constants or a cited theorem whose hypotheses have been verified for the constructed regulator.
